Executive Summary Board-Level

Youna is an AI-native mental wellness and companion platform — not a therapy replacement, but a deeply personalized emotional support companion, wellness coach, and reflective thinking partner that grows more useful the more a user engages with it.

The platform sits at the intersection of three converging markets: the AI companion wave (Replika, Character.ai, Kindroid, Nomi, Pi — estimated $350M–$1.2B TAM), the digital mental health market (Wysa, Youper, BetterHelp — $3.3B–$7.9B TAM), and the telehealth incumbents (Teladoc/BetterHelp, Talkspace — hundreds of millions in revenue).

No competitor combines all four of Youna's differentiators: a multi-framework Personality DNA engine (Big Five + Attachment + HEXACO + Enneagram + DISC + Love Languages + Emotional/Cognitive/Relationship/Behavioral/Motivation/Wellness DNA), a CBT/DBT/ACT/EFT/Mindfulness/SFBT therapeutic framework layer, a multi-layer memory architecture inspired by Kindroid's cascaded memory and Nomi's Identity Core, and a non-therapy positioning with strong safety architecture from day one.

Why Now

  • Market timing: AI companions hit mainstream (Character.ai 20M MAU, Replika 40M+ users, Pi 22K H100 GPUs) — but monetization is broken (Pi: $0 revenue from $1.3B raised; Character.ai: $50M revenue on $250M inference spend; Lovon: $4.17/mo unsustainable on voice-heavy models).
  • Therapy gap: Woebot shut down B2C in June 2025 — no FDA pathway for AI mental health, B2C unsustainable without authorization. Wysa survives with B2B/NHS. The consumer market is hungry for mental wellness tools that are not clinical and not therapy-replacement.
  • Personalization gap: Every competitor uses either rule-based experience (Woebot, Youper — stagnant, can't evolve), context-window + memory (Kindroid, Nomi — impressive but shallow personality), or no memory at all (Character.ai — 2000-word session, no persistent cross-session identity). Youna's multi-framework DNA is unique.
  • Unit economics: With the right model selection (cheap open-source tier for text, value-tier LLM for depth, capped paid voice), Youna can hit $0.09–0.30/DAU/month vs. Character.ai's $250M/$50M disaster. The key insight: text-first, voice-optional, tiered by value, B2B-first for margin.

Product Vision

Vision Statement

Youna becomes the AI-native mental wellness companion that millions trust for daily emotional support, self-understanding, and personal growth — one that knows you deeply, stays with you consistently, and always knows its boundaries.

Mission

Give every person a dependable, privacy-first, emotionally intelligent companion for everyday mental wellness — one that never claims to be a therapist, never oversteps, and never fails silently.

North Star Metric

Weekly Active Companionship Minutes — minutes of meaningful AI-human interaction per active user per week. Drives retention, depth, and eventual willingness to pay. Target: 45 min/week by end of Year 1.

Product Principle #1

Companion, not therapist. Every feature, every prompt, every UI decision reinforces "wellness companion" — never "clinical tool," never "replacement for therapy." This is the positioning that lets us avoid Woebot's trap.

Product Principle #2

Memory is the moat. The most defensible product in this space is the one whose AI genuinely knows the user over time. Kindroid and Nomi proved memory creates stickiness. Youna goes further with multi-framework DNA + identity model.

Product Principle #3

Safety is not a feature — it's the foundation. Crisis detection, delusion guardrails, emotional dependence monitoring, GDPR-by-design, human escalation: these are not "add safety later." They are in the architecture from day 1.

Product Principle #4

Monetize from the start — but never exploit vulnerability. Free tier is generous. Premium is genuinely better. Voice is a paid feature because it costs real money. B2B is the margin engine. No dark patterns, no pushing therapy-seekers to pay.

05 Personality DNA Layer — 7 Axes × 13 Sub-Dimensions

Why this layer exists

Youna's leading differentiator. No competitor runs a structured multi-framework personality engine. Character.ai's personality is user-authored (user writes the persona); Replika's is learned implicitly (brittle); Kindroid's 47 param customizer is shallow surface-level; Nomi's is emergent from chat (no assessment). Youna builds a structured, measured, continuously updating personality model as the core of personalization — not an onboarding checkbox.

How Youna avoids becoming a clinical tool by accident

Three guardrails keep the framework layer in the companion lane:

  1. Language, not protocol. Youna uses framework-consistent phrasing in reflection and questions. It does not run framework protocols (no guided CBT session, no DBT skills group, no ACT values-exercise sequence, no EFT marker-task matching, no SFBT structured session). A framework-consistent phrase is "What's another way to look at that thought?" A protocol would be "Let's do a thought record together now — step 1, identify the situation…" Youna does the former, not the latter.
  2. Reflection + question, not correction. Youna's CBT-consistent language explores thoughts from multiple angles. It does not tell the user their thought is distorted or wrong. It does not assign homework. It does not structure a behavioral experiment for the user. It can ask "What would you expect to happen if that thought were true?" — that's exploration. It cannot say "Your thought is a cognitive distortion; here's the reframe." That's therapy.
  3. Crisis → resources, not framework. When distress is high or crisis is detected, framework language recedes. The crisis protocol takes over: acknowledge, care, resources, step back. No CBT reframe, no ACT values, no DBT skills in a crisis. The companion lane and the crisis lane do not mix.

08 Feature Impact Analysis — Multi-Framework Scoring

Why this section exists — and why it matters for funding

A board and investors don't fund feature lists. They fund prioritization discipline. This section applies five independent frameworks to the same feature set, then reconciles them. When RICE, Kano, MoSCoW, WSJF, and Impact×Effort all point the same direction, you have conviction. When they diverge, you've found a real decision worth surfacing. The dependency impact matrix then maps each feature's downstream effect — so you can see not just "what to build" but "what breaks if you build (or skip) this."

8.1 Scoring Methodology

RICE — Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort

RICE Score = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort
Reach: users reached per month (monthly active)
Impact: 3 = massive, 2 = high, 1 = medium, 0.5 = low, 0.25 = minimal
Confidence: 100% = high (data-backed), 80% = medium, 50% = low (guess)
Effort: person-months to build (dev + design + QA)

Kano — Must-Be / Performance / Delighter / Reject

Must-Be (M): expected; absence causes dissatisfaction; presence doesn't delight.
Performance (P): more is better; satisfaction scales with quality/quantity.
Delighter (D): unexpected; presence delights; absence doesn't dissatisfy.
Reject (R): actively harms — either by its existence or by failing users' core need.

WSJF — Weighted Shortest Job First

WSJF = (User Benefit + Time Criticality + Risk Reduction / Opportunity Enablement) / Job Size
User Benefit: business + user value (1–10)
Time Criticality: urgency to capture value before competitors / market window (1–10)
Risk Reduction / Opp. Enablement: how much this reduces risk or unlocks future (1–10)
Job Size: person-months

MoSCoW — Must / Should / Could / Won't (this cycle)

Must (M): non-negotiable for launch / for the next phase to be viable.
Should (S): important, valuable, but the phase can ship without it (with a known gap).
Could (C): desirable if time/capacity allows — nice-to-have.
Won't (W): explicitly out for this cycle — either deferred, out of scope, or wrong-fit.

Impact × Effort (2×2) — Visual Priority

High Impact / Low Effort = Quick Wins (do first, celebrate)
High Impact / High Effort = Strategic Bets (invest, sequence, commit)
Low Impact / Low Effort = Fillers (do if time, don't prioritize)
Low Impact / High Effort = Traps (avoid — high cost, low return)
Confidence calibration

Features with real user data or clear competitive evidence are Confidence = 100%. Features that depend on user behavior we can't observe yet are 80%. Features that are directional bets on unproven demand are 50%. Honest low confidence scores are better than inflated high ones — they tell investors you know what you don't know.

Youna's economic architecture — what makes the model work
  1. Text-first, affordable-model, voice-optional. Text on GPT-4.1-mini/Claude Haiku/gemini-flash-level models costs $0.11-0.30/DAU/mo — viable at $9.99/mo. Voice is a paid-feature add-on, capped, on professional STT/TTS (not speech-to-speech which is 10× more expensive).
  2. Tiered by value, not by usage. Free tier gets companion + basic tracking. Premium gets memory + DNA + insights. Pro gets coaching + voice unlimited. The value tiers align with what users will pay for — and with what's economically viable to provide.
  3. B2B as the economics-reform lever. At $100/seat/yr, B2B provides accessible margin (LLM bill is <8% of revenue at typical usage). B2B is where the gross margin climbs to 70-80%. Consumer subscription alone is likely to be tight; B2B makes the business model work.
  4. Annual billing for LTV. 17% annual discount is a retention + LTV lever. LTV/CAC improves dramatically with annual retention.
  5. Model routing as cost optimization. Youna can route simple conversations to cheaper models and reserve expensive models for complex ones — the cost/quality balance shifts over time as model pricing evolves.
  6. Caps on expensive modalities. Voice is capped on Premium, unlimited on Pro. Speech-to-speech (the most expensive modality) is not a Phase 3-4 priority — it's a Phase 5 research question.

10.4 B2B Economics — The Path to Margin

B2B is not an "also" revenue stream for Youna — it's a strategic necessity for the financial model to work at scale. Consumer AI companionship subscription businesses, on the evidence, are mostly money-losing when the LLM is expensive and free-tier conversion is low. B2B provides:

B2B risks — be honest about these
  1. Sales cycle. B2B sales take 3-9 months. Year 1 has no B2B revenue. Year 2 has pilots. Year 3 has meaningful revenue. This is a slow ramp, not a quick win.
  2. Compliance cost. B2B requires GDPR/SOC 2/HIPAA-pathway compliance, SSO, data processing agreements, security documentation. This is real engineering + legal cost — $50-150K+ to be enterprise-ready.
  3. Single-customer dependency risk. If one large B2B customer is >20% of revenue, churn kills the business. B2B revenue must be diversified across many small/medium customers, not dependent on a few enterprise deals.
  4. Not a therapy platform. Youna's B2B value proposition is "employee wellness companion + aggregate reporting" — not "therapy provider." Employers who want therapy will go to Lyra/Spring Health/benefits providers. Youna's B2B pitch must be clear about this distinction or it will be confused with (and lose to) therapy platforms.

  3. Regulatory positioning is compromised. If Youna's marketing or product positioning crosses into "therapy," "treatment," "diagnosis," or "clinical" territory in a way that creates regulatory exposure, fix the positioning before scaling. Woebot's trap was real — don't repeat it.
  4. Retention is structurally broken. If after Phase 3-4 users simply don't come back (WAU/MAU < 0.2, 30-day retention < 5%), the product isn't delivering value. Diagnose why (continuity? habit? payoff? engagement?) and fix — or stop if the core value isn't there.
  5. Safety incident at scale. If a safety incident occurs at meaningful scale (harm to a user, data breach, privacy violation), the response determines whether it's a "pause and fix" or a "stop." If the safety infrastructure wasn't in place, it's a stop. If it was and the incident was an edge case, it's a pause + fix + re-evaluate.
Overall verdict — CONDITIONAL GO

Youna is a conditional go. The conditions are:

  1. Phase 3 must complete — real LLM, real memory, real DNA, real frameworks, real safety, real tiers, first revenue. This is the gate. Without Phase 3, Youna is a mock-up, not a product.
  2. B2B must ramp in Phase 4 — without B2B, the consumer-only economics are likely to be tight or unviable. B2B is the economics-reform lever.
  3. Safety must be real before scale — crisis detection, resources, escalation, delusion guardrails, emotional-dependence monitoring. Not a feature — the foundation.
  4. Non-therapy positioning must hold — companion + coach + DNA + safety, not therapist + treatment + diagnosis. Woebot's trap is the cautionary tale.
  5. Funding must be available — Phase 3 ($230K) + Phase 4 ($490K) = ~$720K to get to a scaled, revenue-generating product. Seed + Series A is the funding path. Don't build Phase 5 without Phase 3-4 delivering evidence.

If those conditions are met, Youna has a real path to a differentiated, defensible, profitable AI wellness companion platform. If they're not, the project should pause and re-evaluate — not push forward into an unviable model.

Emotional dependence is a real risk for any companion. Youna's monitoring is informed by this — it doesn't eliminate the risk, but it mitigates it.

Automated metrics (detection rates, content flags, escalation usage) + periodic human review (safety lead + clinical advisor review a sample of interactions) + user feedback loop (safety feedback channel) Phase 5 Continuous safety auditing is the only way to maintain safety at scale. The research doesn't have a direct precedent for a full safety-audit pipeline in AI companions — this is Youna's infrastructure investment. It's expensive, but it's necessary for responsible operation at scale.
Hard truth about safety — no system is perfect

The research is clear: no AI system is safe 100% of the time. Ash (2024) found LLM-as-judge gets to 100% sensitivity / 99.2% specificity / 0.38% failure rate — that's the best result in the literature, and it still has a 0.38% failure rate. Stamatis et al. (2025) found all LLMs exhibit psychogenic cognition and 39.8% of scenarios had no safety interventions. SIM-VAIL (2025) identified dependence-promotion as a risk dimension that no system has solved. Youna's safety architecture reduces risk — it does not eliminate it. The goal is to be as safe as possible, to have the infrastructure to catch and respond to failures, and to be transparent with users, investors, and regulators about what the safety system can and can't do. Safety is a continuous investment, not a one-time build.

The hardest safety question — and Youna's answer

Question: "Is it ethical to build an AI companion that users form emotional attachments to, knowing that (a) it's not human, (b) it can't truly care, (c) it can be turned off or discontinued, and (d) it may create a false sense of having a reliable relationship?"

(5) User agency — users can leave, delete their data, correct the model, and take breaks. (6) Ongoing ethical review — the ethics of AI companionship are evolving, and Youna's approach evolves with them. The ethical risk is real — but it's managed, not ignored.

What's been learned (from the Deep Dive Research + build)

  1. The companion market is real but brutal. AI companions have massive user bases (Character.ai 20M MAU, Replika 40M+ registered) but terrible unit economics (Character.ai spends ~$250M/yr on inference for ~$50M revenue; Pi raised $1.3B and has effectively zero revenue; Lovon's $4.17/mo pricing can't cover voice LLM costs at 3% conversion). The lesson: a companion app without a deliberate economic architecture will burn capital. Youna's architecture (text-first/affordable-model, tiered-by-value pricing, B2B margin, capped voice) is designed to avoid this trap.
  2. Memory is the retention lever. Kindroid's 5-layer cascaded memory (the deepest in the category) and Nomi's Identity Core (the most sophisticated personality model) are the two most impressive architecture patterns in the competitive landscape. Youna's 5-layer memory architecture (conversation log + vector + compressed knowledge + salience-weighted retrieval + dynamic identity model) combines both. Without memory, Youna is a one-session companion. With memory, it's "yours."
  3. Personality DNA is the differentiation. No competitor runs a structured multi-framework personality engine. Character.ai's personality is user-authored (user writes the persona). Replika's is learned implicitly (brittle — 2.0 rebuild broke trust). Kindroid's 47-param customizer is shallow surface-level (not assessed, not measured). Nomi's is emergent from chat (no assessment). Youna's 7-axis/13-sub-dimension DNA (Big Five + HEXACO + Enneagram + DISC + Love Languages + Attachment + Emotional + Cognitive + Relationship + Motivation + Behavioral + Wellness + dynamic signals + LoRA archetype adapters) is the deepest personality architecture in the category. This is the #1 differentiator.
  4. Safety is a gate, not a feature. The research is clear: AI companions have real safety risks (crisis response failure, delusion reinforcement, emotional dependence, harmful content). Ash (2024) found LLM-as-judge gets to 100% sensitivity / 99.2% specificity / 0.38% failure rate — the best result in the literature, and it still has a 0.38% failure rate. Stamatis et al. (2025) found all LLMs exhibit psychogenic cognition and 39.8% of scenarios had no safety interventions. Youna's safety architecture (multi-layer detection, LLM-as-judge, proactive SI checking, delusion guardrails, emotional-dependence monitoring, safety-audit pipeline) is designed to reduce risk — not eliminate it (no system can). Safety is built before launch at scale, not after.
  5. B2B is the economics-reform lever. Consumer AI companionship subscription businesses are, on the evidence, mostly money-losing when the LLM is expensive and free-tier conversion is low. B2B provides accessible margin ($100/seat/yr, LLM bill <8% of revenue), lower churn, lower CAC (at scale), enterprise credibility, and a path to gross margin 70-80%. Youna's financial model depends on B2B — it's not optional.
  6. Non-therapy positioning is both a product choice and a compliance choice. Woebot's trap: B2C mental health app without FDA authorization → shut down B2C. Youna's strategy: stay in the companion lane (wellness companion, not therapist; emotional support, not treatment; reflection, not diagnosis). This avoids the hardest regulatory categories (FDA medical device authorization, high-risk EU AI Act classification) and keeps the compliance burden manageable (GDPR, CCPA, EU AI Act transparency, SOC 2 for B2B, age-gating, privacy).
  7. Some things are traps, not opportunities. Community layer (Replika and Character.ai both learned this — toxicity, moderation burden, poor retention signal). Social ecosystem integration (social APIs are hostile, privacy risk is extreme). Predictive wellness AI without ethical clarity (can cross into "Youna tells me what's going to happen" — not the companion lane). Multi-agent team without clear user need (most users want one coherent companion). Youna has explicitly rejected these — recorded for investors and engineers.
